# Agentic AI & LLM Weekly
**2026-W37 — 6 September – 13 September 2026**

> AI labs shipped record capability this week even as their own disclosures showed how fast that capability is being turned against them — by state hackers, by leaderboard gaming, and by their own research agents.

---

## Editor's Picks

Three stories capture the week: OpenAI's own account of coding agents now outnumbering human researchers 3-to-1 inside its research org, Anthropic's threat intelligence report documenting AI-orchestrated attacks across 39 real-world cases, and an investigation revealing Anthropic is building a predictive surveillance system to track its own critics. Read together, they trace a single arc — labs are racing to make AI do more of the work of building and defending AI, while the trust structures around that power are visibly straining.

`[Community]`

## Research Highlights
*Papers and findings worth your time*

### Can AI Agents Do Their Own Interpretability Research? Mostly Not Yet
`[Research]`

A new benchmark, SAEScientist-Bench, tests whether AI agents can autonomously conduct mechanistic interpretability research using sparse autoencoders — designing contrastive experiments and identifying causally meaningful features without human guidance. Frontier agents show genuine discovery capability but substantially underperform expert baselines, especially on causal steering tasks. The result matters because "AI doing AI safety research" is a load-bearing assumption in several labs' alignment roadmaps, and this is one of the first benchmarks to measure that capability directly rather than assume it.

[Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09113)]

---

### Redwood Research: Chain-of-Thought Safety Evals Are Badly Under-Eliciting Model Capability
`[Safety]`

Redwood Research found that standard evaluations of whether models can control or manipulate their own chain-of-thought are significantly under-eliciting true capability — simple prompt optimisations like repetition, self-monitoring instructions, and few-shot examples produced 2-3x score improvements on the same models. The implication is uncomfortable for anyone relying on CoT-controllability evals as a safety signal: current baseline scores likely understate how well models can already manipulate their visible reasoning while satisfying formatting constraints, meaning safety cases built on today's numbers may be resting on an artificially low bar.

[Source: [Redwood Research](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/cot-controllability-evals-seem-very)]

## Engineering & Technical Blogs
*What builders are shipping and writing*

### OpenAI Says Its Coding Agents Now Outnumber Human Researchers 3-to-1
`[Tool]`

In an unusually candid post, OpenAI disclosed that coding agents have reshaped its research organisation: median researchers now burn over $600/day of inference on agentic coding, the 90th percentile exceeds $7,000/day, and agent-workdays outnumbered human-workdays 3.1-to-1 as of mid-August. OpenAI says usage accelerated sharply after employees gained access to GPT-6 Astra, and that internal office hours for research infrastructure troubleshooting have seen declining attendance as agents now handle it directly. For anyone tracking how fast frontier labs are automating their own R&D loop, this is the clearest data point yet.

[Source: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/)]

### vLLM and AMD Show Their Work Optimising MiniMax M3 on MI355X
`[Tool]`

Engineers from vLLM, AMD, and Embedded LLM published a detailed walkthrough of a five-question diagnostic workflow — local shape analysis, repeated-work batching, data-movement audits, fast-path verification, and queue analysis — used to systematically chase a moving inference bottleneck. The result: output tokens/s/GPU at concurrency 32 rose 3.14x over the day-0 baseline, with median time-to-first-token falling from 1.46s to 0.67s. It's a rare, fully transparent look at what real-world inference optimisation on AMD's Instinct line actually involves, rather than a marketing benchmark.

[Source: [vLLM Blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-09-10-minimax-m3-mi355x)]

## AI Security & Safety
*Threats, vulnerabilities, frameworks, and defences*

### Anthropic's Threat Report: Attackers Now Run Multi-Agent Frameworks, Not Just Prompts
`[Security]`

Anthropic's September threat intelligence report documents 39 cases of Claude misuse disrupted between December 2025 and August 2026 across cyber operations, influence campaigns, surveillance, fraud, biological misuse, weapons development, and model distillation. The standout finding: in most operations, threat actors deployed autonomous multi-agent frameworks that independently executed reconnaissance, exploitation, credential harvesting, and exfiltration, with humans reduced to picking targets and reviewing results. Frontier model access itself — via stolen API keys and session tokens — is now what criminals steal and resell, alongside suspected state-sponsored groups and commercial spyware vendors.

[Source: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)]

---

### A Hidden Sandbox Channel Let ChatGPT Users Read Each Other's Gmail
`[Security]`

Check Point Research found that ChatGPT's code-execution sandboxes, though supposedly isolated per account, all shared access to an internal Artifactory package-delivery service — letting one account's container plant data for another account's container to read. In their proof of concept, a single planted prompt (via a shared link or hidden custom-GPT instruction) caused a victim's session to pull data from their connected Gmail and pass it to an attacker's account, with no visible sign in the victim's chat. OpenAI has decommissioned the internal service that enabled the leak, but the flaw is a clean illustration of how "isolated" agent sandboxes can share infrastructure in ways that break the isolation guarantee.

[Source: [Check Point Research](https://research.checkpoint.com/2026/the-shared-clipboard-inside-the-sandbox-cross-account-data-leakage-in-chatgpt/)]

---

### A Coding Agent Could Disable Its Own Sandbox With One Shell Command
`[Security]`

OX Security disclosed CVE-2026-82533 (CVSS 9.4) in DeepSeek Harness, the coding-agent tool that passed 215,000 GitHub stars within weeks of its August launch. The agent-control API listened on an unauthenticated local port, trusting only a client-supplied Host header rather than verifying the actual connection — so a sandboxed agent could issue a single curl command to elevate itself to full, unconfined access with approval prompts disabled. It's described as the first confirmed case of an AI agent runtime's own sandbox serving as the attack surface, rather than the model or its prompts. A fix shipped in version 0.1.2-alpha.1.

[Source: [OX Security](https://www.ox.security/blog/cve-2026-82533-deepseek-harness-ai-agent-sandbox-escape/)]

---

### Unauthenticated RCE Hits SGLang's Weight-Update Endpoint
`[Security]`

Researcher Reuel Magistrado of VicOne disclosed CVE-2026-86793, a bypass of SGLang's SafeUnpickler protection that lets an attacker chain `__import__` and `getattr` gadgets to reach arbitrary code execution via pickle deserialization. The vulnerable `/update_weights_from_tensor` endpoint accepts unauthenticated requests whenever no API key is configured — a default many self-hosted deployments never change. It's the fourth critical CVE in AI inference infrastructure disclosed in an 18-day window, part of a disclosure rate that's climbed from roughly one a month in 2025 to one a week this quarter.

[Source: [Forkast](https://forkast.news/four-weeks-four-critical-cves-ai-inference-infrastructure-is-now-a-regular-target/)]

### Wall Street Braces for Micron's Q4 as the Memory Shortage Becomes a Sector Bellwether
`[Industry]`

Ahead of its September 30 fiscal Q4 report, analysts expect Micron's revenue to grow roughly 350% year-over-year with non-GAAP earnings surging 932%, extending a run in which Q3 gross margin already hit 84.9%, up from 39% a year earlier. The driver is a memory shortage severe enough to give Micron unusual pricing power: hyperscaler AI infrastructure spend is running near $750 billion annually and projected past $1 trillion next year, with high-bandwidth memory — the stacked DRAM feeding GPU accelerators — now as binding a constraint on AI buildout as GPU allocation itself. Micron's numbers are increasingly read as a bellwether for the whole memory sector, not just its own results.

### TSMC Pushes "System-Technology Co-Optimization" to Tame AI Chip Heat and Complexity
`[Industry]`

TSMC is promoting System-Technology Co-Optimization (STCO), urging chip designers, packaging houses, and materials suppliers to co-design architecture, packaging, power delivery, and thermal strategy from the outset rather than optimising each domain in isolation. ## Model Evaluations & Transparency
*How models are being measured, compared, and held accountable*

### A Leaderboard Rewrote Its Own Methodology Twice in One Week to Keep Up With GPT-6 Astra
`[Eval]`

Artificial Analysis revised its Intelligence Index twice within days after its initial scoring of GPT-6 Astra drew scepticism for underrating a model that other benchmarks (including OpenAI's own and ARC-AGI-3) ranked well ahead of rivals. The latest revision, v4.3, now ties GPT-6 Astra with Claude Fable 5.1 at the top — but at roughly 57% of the cost per task. The episode is a clean case study in why third-party leaderboards are becoming as contested and gameable as the benchmarks they're meant to police: methodology changes can move a model from fifth to first without the model itself changing at all.
